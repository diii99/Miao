# Xijiang terrain and slope source notes

Researched 2026-09-09 for the requested terrain correction. This note distinguishes valley-scale topography from individual house foundations. It does not assign a measured slope angle where a source provides none.

## Village-specific evidence

1. **Peking University River Basin Human Settlements Research Center, teaching case YA-028 (2025-07-10).** [Case](https://rbhrc.pku.edu.cn/info/1208/2961.htm). Describes a river valley at approximately 800 m elevation, a gentler northeast slope and a steeper southwest cliff, with relief of several hundred metres. The river separates northeast and southwest settlement areas. Main streets follow the river and slope foot; secondary lanes climb across contours. This supports asymmetric valley sides, not a uniformly steep bowl. Confidence: useful qualitative synthesis; approximate elevation and relief, no measured angle or survey benchmarks. Its bibliography includes the field/GIS study below.

2. **Guizhou Provincial People's Congress, scenic-area reopening report (2020-03-30).** [Report](https://www.gzrd.gov.cn/gzwh/202003/t20200330_77670475.html). The scenic rice-field area exceeds 400 mu and is stated to lie at 850–900 m elevation. This is a useful numeric check for the fields below Dongyin, but must not be treated as the elevation range of the entire village or the lookout. Confidence: government-hosted specific elevation range; no reference datum or survey methodology supplied.

3. **Xu Qian, Li Yangbing and Huang Juan (2020), Journal of Henan Normal University, 48(6):10–18, DOI 10.16366/j.cnki.1000-2367.2020.06.002.** [University article](https://www.htu.edu.cn/nature/2023/0817/c19685a280364/page.htm), [full paper](https://www.htu.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/40/2d/d47354214172b86bf725cfb84bc1/cbd49aaa-eaba-4630-b599-4183a32cde88.pdf). Uses imagery and fieldwork to map village development. Older two- and three-storey buildings concentrate on the northeast river-valley slope; later growth extends along the river and roads. Figures map buildings, roads and land uses, but do not establish a numeric terrain slope. Confidence: original village-scale GIS/field research for settlement layout, not a DEM-slope dataset.

4. **《千家苗寨：西江苗人的日常生活》, “西江苗人的干栏建筑”, published 2013-04-01.** [Guizhou Digital Publishing chapter](https://www.yuncunzhai.com/article/210971.jhtml). The village-specific ethnographic description reports house sites on 30–70 degree slopes and two-tier house platforms with roughly 2 m height difference, 3.5 m depth, and approximately 100 m² total platform area. These values describe local house-site adaptation; no slope measurement method, mapped sampling extent or distribution is supplied. They are suitable references for stepped foundation construction and variable stilt lengths, **not proof that the whole inhabited hillside averages 30–70 degrees**.

## Regional evidence: do not apply as a village measurement

5. **Yu Meng et al. (2022), Geographical Research 41(8):2184–2202, DOI 10.11821/dlyj020210841.** [Original paper](https://www.dlyj.ac.cn/CN/abstract/article/1000-0585/52277). Study area is the full 178.98 km² historic Xijiang township, not just the scenic village. A 30 m DEM supplies slopes; over 89% of township rice fields fall at 713–1113 m. Township terrain is generally higher southeast and lower northwest. This supports a terrain-derived approach but cannot specify the village lookout height or individual hillside angles.

## Public DEM sources and interpretation

- **Copernicus GLO-30 on AWS:** [dataset](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/), [provider processing documentation](https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/readme.html). Free public COGs; this mirror uses the 2021 release. It is a **digital surface model** including buildings and vegetation, so terrain sampled in a wooded or built village can sit above bare ground. At Xijiang latitude the tile has 3600 × 3600 samples per degree, approximately 28 m east–west and 31 m north–south. The documented filename template gives [N26 E108 COG](https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N26_00_E108_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N26_00_E108_00_DEM.tif); binary availability must be verified when retrieving. The `10` token denotes one arc second, not ten metres. Documented pixel registration matters when sampling: the mirror removes duplicated east/south edge samples.

- **Mapzen / Tilezen terrain on AWS:** [provider data sources](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/data-sources.md), [provider formats and endpoints](https://www.mapzen.com/blog/terrain-tile-service/). Global midlatitude land source is SRTM; provider describes 30 m samples with 90 m nominal quality. Terrarium PNG decodes to metres as `(R * 256 + G + B / 256) - 32768`. Endpoint: `https://s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`. High zoom tiles can be resampled from coarser source data, so pixel density must not be presented as survey precision. The provider documents an `X-Imagery-Sources` response header that can identify contributing source files when present.

## Modelling implication

Use real sampled elevation differences and horizontal metre distances to compute `atan(rise / run)`, keep equal vertical/horizontal scale for terrain, and report the source resolution. Preserve the broad lower river/field floor, gentler northeast inhabited slope and steeper southwest lookout side. Re-seat buildings on stepped pads and re-evaluate stair/road inclines after the terrain changes. Do not globally steepen to an ethnographic maximum of 70 degrees, confuse percentage grade with degrees, or claim individual foundations are surveyed from a roughly 30 m DSM.

No verified source in this bounded search supplies a numeric lookout elevation, maximum inhabited-village elevation, or single measured angle for the village as a whole. Those should come from the sampled DEM with an explicit point/area footprint rather than travel-copy assertions.
